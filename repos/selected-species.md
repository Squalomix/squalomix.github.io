# Selected species

Among the [species we work on](../species.md), a small number anchor the consortium's work: for these species we have generated data well beyond a single genome assembly — transcriptomes, gene expression profiles, karyotype data and other resources. For three of them, the brownbanded bamboo shark, the whale shark and the cloudy catshark, we have released a succession of genome assemblies over the years, summarised in the tables below so that the assembly level, the technology used and the corresponding publication can be told apart at a glance.

## Brownbanded bamboo shark *Chiloscyllium punctatum*

One of the two egg-laying species we chose first, to facilitate sustainable sampling without sacrificing adults while also supporting laboratory experimentation. Alongside the assemblies, a Trinity transcriptome assembly, curated gene models and a [gene expression profile](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Chilocyllium_punctatum_gene_expression_profiles_v1/20549634) (figshare) are available. The expression profile is based on read mapping onto the non-chromosomal, scaffold-level assembly available at the time of Hara et al., 2018; re-quantification on the latest chromosome-scale assembly is in preparation. Its karyotype is 2n = 106 (see [Karyotypes](karyotype.md)). CTCF ChIP-seq data obtained for this species have also been released (DDBJ Sequence Read Archive, DRA006338).

## Whale shark *Rhincodon typus*

The world's largest fish, and a long-standing subject of our collaboration with public aquariums, which made repeated sampling of this endangered species possible. Its genome has been assembled three times in succession, each time improving contiguity and completeness, and transcriptome data accompany the assemblies.

| Assembly | Level | Technology | Publication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rtypus_kobe_v1.0 ([figshare](https://figshare.com/projects/sharkgenome1-phyloinfokobe/28863)) | Scaffold | Illumina short reads + mate-pair | [Hara et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-018-0673-5) |
| [sRhiTyp1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/assembly/GCA_021869965.1/) (GCA_021869965.1) | Chromosome | 10X Linked Reads + Hi-C scaffolding | [Yamaguchi et al., 2023](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/33/9/1527) |
| [sRhiTyp1.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/assembly/GCA_021869965.2/) (GCA_021869965.2) | Chromosome | Long reads (ONT) + Hi-C | [Kawaguchi et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giag014) |

We also contributed our data production to a parallel effort on an individual reared in Georgia Aquarium, US, with Milton Tan and his collaborators ([Tan et al., eLife, 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.65394)).

## Cloudy catshark *Scyliorhinus torazame*

The other of our two founding egg-laying species, and a main laboratory model of the consortium. A Trinity transcriptome assembly and a [gene expression profile](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Scyliorhinus_torazame_gene_expression_profiles_v1/20549586) (figshare) accompany the genome assemblies. The expression profile is based on read mapping onto the non-chromosomal, scaffold-level assembly available at the time of Hara et al., 2018; re-quantification on the latest chromosome-scale assembly is in preparation. Its karyotype is 2n = 64 (see [Karyotypes](karyotype.md)). CTCF ChIP-seq data for this species are also publicly available (DDBJ Sequence Read Archive, DRA006338).

|---|---|---|---|

## Zebra shark *Stegostoma tigrinum*

A flagship oviparous species for our chromosome-scale work. Its assembly sSteFas1.1 served as a central case in our study of karyotype evolution in elasmobranchs ([Yamaguchi et al., 2023](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/33/9/1527)), and its karyotype (2n = 102) was documented by our own cell culture work; see [Karyotypes](karyotype.md).

## Megamouth shark *Megachasma pelagios*

One of the rarest large sharks, known from very few specimens. Despite the scarcity of material, we have accumulated transcriptome reads as well as genomic reads from multiple platforms (short reads, linked reads, PacBio HiFi and Hi-C), making it one of our most extensively sampled species relative to the availability of specimens. Sequencing status is summarised in the [Species list](../species.md).

## Red stingray *Hemitrygon akajei*

Our flagship batoid, representing the skate and ray lineage alongside the shark species above. Its HiFi + Hi-C data underlie our analysis of genome size dynamics across elasmobranchs ([Kuraku et al., 2026](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/36/8/1708)), and it is one of the species for which chromosome-scale assemblies and karyotype data are both in hand; see [Species](../species.md) and [Karyotypes](karyotype.md).
